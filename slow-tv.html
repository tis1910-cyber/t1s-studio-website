SLOW TV / T1S

Stay long enough
to notice.

Slow TV is not the absence of events. It changes the scale of what counts as an event.

00:00:00

03:00:00+

No second-by-second editing.

I found Slow TV while I was already filming and publishing T1S videos. What surprised me was how naturally it matched what I enjoyed: letting the camera stay, letting a place breathe and not feeling that every quiet second had to be removed.

“You start appreciating the micro-events.”

A sheep looking up can become an event. A horse wandering into frame can become the scene. Wind moving across long grass, a bird landing, an animal nudging the camera — these tiny moments become visible when the video gives them enough time.

A window into somewhere else.

There is something slightly mysterious about a fixed camera left in another place. You are not being told where to look every few seconds. You can observe. You can ignore it. You can return ten minutes later and see what changed.

That makes Slow TV flexible. Some people may actively watch it. Others may leave it on a television while reading, working, resting or sharing a room. It can become a digital window rather than a conventional “show”.

There is no correct use.

T1S videos are made for pleasant time, not a prescribed task. Relax with them. Study. Work. Sleep. Put them on in a waiting room or a care-home lounge. Watch a cow do almost nothing for half an hour. If it works for you, that is enough.
